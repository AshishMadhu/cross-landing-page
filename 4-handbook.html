RAILWAYCROSS · LEVEL-CROSSING STATUS

Know if the gate is open before you get there.

RailwayCross shows every nearby railway level crossing as open, closing or closed, with a countdown to the next train. This page is the short handbook: what it does, how to read it, and how the numbers are made.

Features#

Five tabs, one idea: give you the answer for the gate in front of you.

Home

Verdict home

A full-bleed, status-coloured hero for your saved or nearest crossing: a giant countdown, the train that is coming, and the crossing drawn as a small animated scene.

  • Tap a crossing in the tray to make it the verdict, tap the hero for detail.
  • Navigate opens turn-by-turn directions in your maps app.
  • Location off, offline or empty states fill the same slot.
Nearby

Nearby crossings

A list and a map of every mapped crossing within 20 km, with distance, next train and estimated wait. Search by name.

Trains

Live trains

Live train positions behind the countdowns. Sort by delay, state or number. The list refreshes every 60 seconds.

Timetable

Timetable and planner

Pick a crossing with the verdict strip to see every train passing it. Switch to Planner for today's gate-down windows, an "if you arrive at…" scrubber and a weekly busiest-hours heatmap.

Settings

Settings

Closure and delay alert toggles, theme (system, light or dark) and the crossing illustration style. Everything is stored on your device.

Guides#

Getting started

  1. Install the app on Android or iOS, or open railwaycross.com. There is nothing to sign up for.
  2. Allow location when asked. It is used to find crossings within 20 km of you.
  3. Read the colour. Your nearest crossing fills the Home screen. Green, amber or red tells you the state at a glance.
  4. Save a favourite so it is the first thing you see next time.

If location is off you will see a prompt instead of a verdict. Trains and timetables still work.

Reading the status

The same three colours are used for the hero, pills and map pins, so a status looks the same everywhere.

OpenNo train is expected soon. The countdown is the time to the next one.
ClosingA train is close. The gate is about to come down.
ClosedGate down, train passing. Shows an estimated wait.

Safety first. RailwayCross is a planning aid. Its data can be late or wrong. Never cross against a barrier, signal or siren, and always look and listen.

How estimates work

The backend combines timetables with live running status and works out when each train reaches each crossing on its route, including its current delay. It does not know how long a gate stays down.

So the app assumes each train closes the gate from 1 minute before until 4 minutes after its crossing time, about 5 minutes in all. Wherever the app shows a duration derived from that, it uses "≈" or says "estimates". The planner is computed entirely on your device from the timetable already loaded. Nothing is sent to the server.

Reference#

API

The app is a client of the RailwayCross backend. It uses three public, read-only endpoints, with no key or login. Response shapes may change without notice, so treat this as a guide.

EndpointParameters
GET/api/crossings/nearby/latitude, longitude, radius (km; the app uses 20), with_status=1 to include the verdict
GET/api/crossings/<id>/timetable/Trains passing the crossing, with interpolated crossing times
GET/api/trains/live/sort = delay, state or number
# base URL: https://admin.railwaycross.com
curl -G "https://admin.railwaycross.com/api/crossings/nearby/" \
  -d latitude=9.30 -d longitude=76.59 -d radius=20 -d with_status=1

FAQ#

Is RailwayCross a safety system?

No. It is a planning aid built on timetables and live data, either of which can be wrong. Obey barriers and signals and always look and listen.

How does it know a train is coming?

Timetables and live running status are combined to work out when each train reaches each crossing on its route, delays included.

How long will the gate be down?

The backend does not say, so the app assumes about 5 minutes per train: 1 minute before to 4 minutes after the crossing time. That is why durations carry a "≈".

Do I need an account?

No. Your saved crossing, theme and alert preferences are stored on your device.

What happens to my location?

Your coordinates are sent with the nearby-crossings request so the server can find crossings within 20 km. There is no account to link them to.

Will I get push alerts?

Settings has closure and delay toggles that record your preference. Push delivery is not live yet.

Does it work offline?

Live data needs a connection. Offline, you get a clear offline state instead of a stale answer.

A crossing near me is missing.

Crossings come from the backend's mapped data. If yours is missing, it has not been mapped yet.

Which platforms are supported?

Android and iOS apps, plus a web app at railwaycross.com with nearby crossings, trains and timetables.
