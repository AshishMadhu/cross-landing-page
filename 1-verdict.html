How it works

Three states. No guessing.

The app reads the timetable and live train positions around you and boils it down to the one thing you need at the gate.

1

Find crossings near you

Allow location once and RailwayCross lists every mapped level crossing within 20 km, nearest first.

2

Match the trains

Timetables and live running status are combined to estimate when each train reaches each crossing, delays included.

3

Read the verdict

Green means open, amber means a train is close, red means the gate is down. The countdown tells you how long.

From install to answer in a minute

RailwayCross has no accounts and no sign-up.

  1. Install the app on Android or iOS, or open the web app.
  2. Allow location when asked. It is only used to find crossings within 20 km.
  3. Tap a crossing in the tray to make it the verdict, or tap the hero for full detail.
  4. Save it so it opens first next time.
Location off? The hero shows a prompt instead of a verdict. You can still browse trains and timetables.

Prefer a bigger screen? The same crossings, trains and timetables are on the web at railwaycross.com.

How the planner estimates gates

The backend knows when trains pass a crossing, not how long the gate stays down. So the planner assumes each train closes it from 1 minute before to 4 minutes after the crossing time, and live delays shift those windows.

Everything is computed on your device from the timetable it already loaded. Estimates are marked with "≈" wherever they appear.

  1. Today shows gate-down windows and the open gaps between them.
  2. If you arrive at… lets you scrub to a time and see the verdict for it.
  3. Busiest hours is a weekly heatmap of when gates are down most.

Public, read-only endpoints

The app is a client of the RailwayCross backend. The endpoints it uses need no key or login.

GET /api/crossings/nearby/
latitude, longitude, radius (km, app uses 20), with_status=1 to include verdicts.

GET /api/crossings/<id>/timetable/
Trains passing a crossing with crossing times.

GET /api/trains/live/
sort = delay, state or number.

Response shapes can change. Treat this as a guide, not a contract.
# nearby crossings with status
curl -G "https://admin.railwaycross.com/api/crossings/nearby/" \
  -d latitude=9.30 -d longitude=76.59 -d radius=20 -d with_status=1

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Is RailwayCross a safety system?

No. It is a planning aid built on timetables and live running data, and either can be wrong. Never cross against a barrier, signal or siren, and always look and listen yourself.

How does it know a train is coming?

The backend combines train timetables with live running status, then works out when each train should reach each crossing on its route, including the current delay.

How accurate is the "gate closed" time?

Train arrival times come from data. Gate-down duration is an estimate: we assume about 5 minutes per train. That is why the planner uses "≈" and says "estimates".

Do I need an account?

No. There are no accounts. Your saved crossing, theme and alert preferences are kept on your device.

What happens to my location?

Your coordinates are sent with the nearby-crossings request so the server can find crossings within 20 km. Nothing else about you is collected because there is nothing to sign in to.

Will I get alerts?

Settings has closure and delay alert toggles that record what you want. Push notification delivery is not switched on yet.

Does it work offline?

It needs a connection for live data. Without one you get a clear offline state instead of a stale answer.

A crossing near me is missing.

Crossings come from the backend's mapped data. If one is not there yet, it has not been mapped.

Next time you leave, check the gate first.
