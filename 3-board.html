1Getting started
  1. Install on Android or iOS, or open the web app.
  2. Allow location to find crossings within 20 km.
  3. Tap a crossing in the tray to make it the verdict. Tap the hero for detail.
  4. Save a favourite so it opens first.

No account needed. If location is off, you still get trains and timetables.

3How the planner estimates a gate-down window

The backend knows when a train passes a crossing, not how long the gate stays down. The planner assumes 1 minute before to 4 minutes after the crossing time, and shifts it by the train's live delay. It is computed on your device and marked "≈".

≈ 08:51 – 08:56
08:3008:4509:0009:15

Example: a train due at 08:52 closes the gate from about 08:51 to 08:56.

? Announcements

Passengers are advised.

Q1Is RailwayCross a safety system?

No. It is a planning aid built on timetables and live data, either of which can be wrong. Never cross against a barrier or signal, and always look and listen.

Q2How does it know a train is coming?

Timetables and live running status are combined to work out when each train will reach each crossing, delays included.

Q3How long will the gate be down?

The app estimates about 5 minutes per train. That is an assumption, not backend data, so the planner shows "≈".

Q4Do I need an account?

No. Your saved crossing, theme and alert preferences live on your device.

Q5What happens to my location?

Your coordinates are sent with the nearby-crossings request so the server can find crossings within 20 km. There is no account to link them to.

Q6Will I get push alerts?

Settings has closure and delay toggles that record your preference. Push delivery is not live yet.

Q7Does it work offline?

Live data needs a connection. Offline you get a clear offline state rather than a stale answer.

Q8My crossing is not listed.

Crossings come from the backend's mapped data. If yours is missing, it has not been mapped yet.
